Samsung Speaker Not Working? Fixes for Galaxy

Is your Samsung speaker not working, with no sound from calls, music, or videos? The cause is often simple, and the fix takes only a few minutes. Work through these steps in order, and test the sound after each one.

Why Does a Samsung Speaker Stop Working?

A Galaxy phone has more than one sound setting, so a silent phone can have many causes. The volume may be low for media while the ringtone still plays. Bluetooth may send the sound to earbuds you forgot about. Dust may block the speaker grille.

Software can cause it too. A new app can change the audio, a bug can mute the sound after an update, or one app can send its sound to the wrong device. A damaged speaker is also possible, but it is the least common cause.

Step 1: Raise the Volume and Check Mute

Press the volume up button while music is playing. A Galaxy phone keeps separate volumes for media, calls, and ringtones, so the ringtone can be loud while media volume sits at zero. Drag the media slider up, and check the mute icon is off.

Step 2: Turn Bluetooth Off

Swipe down from the top of the screen and turn Bluetooth off, then play a song. Earbuds, a car stereo, or a smart speaker can stay connected after you stop using them, and the phone keeps sending sound to them. If the sound comes back, a forgotten device was the cause.

Step 3: Turn Off Do Not Disturb

Check the quick panel for the Do Not Disturb icon. When it is on, calls and alerts are silenced, and it is easy to forget you turned it on. Tap it off and test the sound again. A sleep mode can also turn it on by itself.

Step 4: Remove the Case and Screen Protector

Take the case off and play a song. Many cases cover the bottom grille by a small amount, and that is enough to make the sound weak or silent. A screen protector can also hang over the earpiece slit at the top. If the sound comes back, use a case with a proper cutout.

Step 5: Clean the Speaker Grille Gently

Look at the bottom grille in good light. Dust and lint from pockets build up in the small holes, and a thick layer can block the sound almost fully. Brush the grille gently with a soft brush, and wipe it with a dry cloth. Never push a pin into the holes, because sharp tools tear the mesh and can damage the speaker. Sound can also shake dust loose, so try our Samsung Speaker Cleaner, Deep Clean, or Vibration tools.

Step 6: Restart the Phone

Hold the power button and the volume down button together, then tap Restart. A restart clears small software glitches that can mute the speaker, and it closes apps that may be holding the sound. After the phone starts again, play a song. If the sound is back, a stuck app or a small glitch was the cause.

Step 7: Try Safe Mode to Rule Out a Bad App

Safe mode runs only the apps that came with the phone, so it tells you whether an installed app is causing the silence. Hold the power button, then long press Power off until Safe mode appears, and tap it. If the sound works in safe mode, restart to leave it, then remove the newest apps one by one.

Step 8: Check Separate App Sound

If the sound fails in only one app, open Settings, then Sounds and vibration, then Separate app sound. This setting can send one app to a different device, such as a Bluetooth speaker, while the rest of the phone uses its own speaker. Turn it off, or set the app back to This phone, then test the app again.

Step 9: Update the Software

Open Settings, then Software update, and tap Download and install. A software update can fix a sound bug that came with an earlier update. Charge the phone and connect to Wi-Fi first. After the update, restart the phone and test the sound again.

Step 10: Run the Speaker Test in Samsung Members

If the Samsung Members app is installed on your phone, open it and look for Diagnostics. It has a speaker test that plays a tone and asks whether you can hear it. This tells you whether the speaker itself works, which is useful before you decide on a repair.

When It Is Time to See a Repair Shop

If you worked through all ten steps and the speaker is still silent, the speaker itself may be damaged. A torn membrane, water damage, or a loose part inside the phone will not improve with cleaning or with settings. Take the phone to a repair shop, and tell them what you already tried. For more help, read our guide on how to test if your phone speaker is damaged, and our general fixes for a phone speaker not working.

Final Guidance

A Samsung speaker that stops working is usually a small problem with a quick fix. Check the volume, turn off Bluetooth and Do Not Disturb, remove the case, clean the grille, and restart the phone before you worry about damage. Most Galaxy phones sound fine again after one of these steps. If nothing helps, a repair shop can test the speaker and tell you the real cause.
