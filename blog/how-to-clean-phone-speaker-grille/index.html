How to Clean Phone Speaker Grille Safely

How to clean your phone speaker grille safely, without tearing the mesh or pushing dirt deeper inside? The grille is small and delicate, but the clean is simple. This guide shows you the safe steps, and the mistakes to avoid.

What Is the Phone Speaker Grille?

The speaker grille is the row of tiny holes at the bottom of your phone, and the thin slit at the top for the earpiece. Behind the holes sits a fine mesh, and behind the mesh sits the speaker itself. The mesh keeps dust out, but it also catches dust over time, and that dust blocks the sound.

The bottom grille is the one that plays music, videos, and the loudspeaker on calls. The earpiece grille at the top is the one you listen to on a normal call. Both can get blocked, and both need the same gentle care. A clean grille lets the sound pass through clearly, and a dirty one makes the phone sound quiet or muffled.

Signs Your Speaker Grille Needs Cleaning

The most common sign is sound that gets quieter over time. Music sounds flat, calls sound far away, and you keep turning the volume up. If you look at the grille in good light, you may see grey or brown dust sitting in the holes. On light coloured phones the dust is easy to see, and on dark phones you may need a bright light to spot it.

What You Need

You need a soft brush, a dry soft cloth, and good light. A clean soft toothbrush works well, and so does a small makeup brush. The cloth should be dry, because a wet cloth can push water into the grille. You do not need any cleaner, any liquid, or any sharp tool. If you want to clean deeper than a brush can reach, keep our free Speaker Cleaner open in your browser, because sound can shake out dust that sits inside the grille.

Take the case off first, because the case can cover part of the grille and hide the dust. Work on a clean surface, and wash your hands first, so you do not add more dirt while you clean.

Step 1: Brush the Grille Gently

Hold the phone with the grille facing down, so the dust falls out instead of deeper in. Brush across the holes with short, light strokes. Do not press hard, and do not scrub in circles, because that can push the dust into the mesh instead of out of it. Brush the bottom grille first, then turn the phone and brush the earpiece slit at the top in the same gentle way.

Step 2: Use a Dry Cloth

Wipe the area around the grille with the dry cloth, to pick up the dust the brush loosened. Wipe the bottom edge of the phone and the top edge where the earpiece sits. Do not push the cloth into the holes, because the cloth cannot fit and it will only push dust back in.

If the phone has a sticky mark near the grille, dampen a small corner of the cloth very lightly, wipe the mark, and dry the area at once with the dry part of the cloth. Keep the damp part away from the holes themselves, because water in the grille is the one thing you want to avoid.

Step 3: Clean with Sound

A brush can only reach the outside of the grille. Dust that sits deeper inside needs sound. Open our deep dust clean, turn the volume up to the top, and press start. The sweep plays tones that rise from about 200 Hz up to 1500 Hz, and each step shakes the speaker in a different way, so dust stuck in different parts of the grille gets shaken loose.

Hold the phone with the speaker facing down while the tone runs, and let it run for the full time. When it stops, play a song to test the sound. If the sound is clear, the clean worked. If it is still a little muffled, run the sweep once more. Read our guide on how to clean tablet speaker grills for the same steps on a bigger device.

Step 4: Check the Earpiece Grille Too

Many people clean the bottom grille and forget the earpiece at the top. The earpiece grille is a thin slit, and it collects dust just as fast. Brush it gently with the soft brush, in short strokes along the slit, and wipe the area with the dry cloth. Do not push anything into the slit, because it is very narrow and the mesh behind it tears easily.

Mistakes to Avoid

Never use a pin, a needle, a toothpick, or a SIM ejector tool to clean the grille. Sharp tools tear the mesh, and once the mesh is torn, dust goes straight onto the speaker, and the sound gets worse. Never blow into the grille with your mouth, because your breath has moisture in it, and that moisture can leave dirt behind when it dries.

How Often Should You Clean the Grille?

Once a month is enough for most people. Clean it sooner if you carry the phone in a dusty pocket, work outside, or go to the beach, because sand and dust get into the grille fast in those places. A quick brush takes less than a minute, so it is easy to make it a habit.

Final Guidance

Cleaning your phone speaker grille is safe when you keep it gentle. Brush lightly with a soft brush, wipe with a dry cloth, and use sound to shake out the dust that sits deeper inside. Never use pins, water, or strong air, because those are the things that cause damage. A clean grille takes a minute, and it keeps your calls clear and your music loud.
