One Speaker Louder Than the Other? Why and How to Fix

If one speaker is louder than the other on your phone, you are not imagining it. One side can sound clear while the other sounds quiet and dull, and the cause is often simple. This guide walks through the most common reasons, from a balance setting to dust in the grille, and the easy checks you can do at home. If the quiet side sounds muffled, try our free speaker cleaner tool first, because dust is one of the top causes.

First Check: Make Sure the Difference Is Real

Before you fix anything, check that the difference is real and not just how you are holding the phone. Play the same song at the same volume, then turn the phone around and listen again. If the same side stays quiet, the problem is in the phone. If the quiet side changes when you turn it, the problem is how you hold it or the room around you.

Test with the phone on a table too, away from your hands and your pocket. Play music, then play a voice call on speaker. If both sound uneven in the same way, move on to the causes below. A quick check now saves you from fixing the wrong thing.

Cause 1: The Sound Balance Setting Moved

Most phones have a left and right balance setting, and it can move by accident. Open your sound or accessibility settings and look for the balance slider. If it sits to one side, drag it back to the middle. This is the fastest fix of all, and it costs nothing. Check it first, before you clean anything or restart the phone.

Cause 2: Dust or Lint in One Speaker Grille

Dust builds up in one grille more than the other, because of how the phone sits in your pocket or your hand. The blocked side sounds dull and quiet, while the clean side sounds normal. Look at both grilles in good light. If one looks dark or full, run the deep dust clean sweep, which plays tones that shake dust loose from the grille. Wipe the grille with a dry soft brush after the sweep, then test the sound again.

Cause 3: One Earbud Is Quieter Than the Other

If the problem only shows up with wireless earbuds, the phone speaker may be fine. One earbud can run low on battery, fall out of sync, or collect earwax in its mesh. Charge both earbuds fully, then pair them again from scratch. Clean the mesh of the quiet earbud with a dry soft brush. If the earbuds still sound uneven, test the phone speaker on its own to be sure where the problem sits.

Cause 4: How You Hold the Phone and the Room Around You

Your hand can cover one speaker grille without you noticing it, and that side will sound quiet. A wall or a soft sofa on one side can also soak up sound. Hold the phone with both grilles clear, then place it on a hard flat table in the middle of the room and listen again. If the sound evens out, there was never a fault, only the way you held it.

Cause 5: A Speaker That Is Starting to Fail

A speaker can wear out slowly, and one side fails first. The signs are a quiet side that stays quiet after cleaning, a buzz or a rattle at high volume, or sound that cuts in and out. Run the simple checks in our guide on how to test if your phone speaker is damaged to confirm it. If the tests point to damage, a repair shop needs to open the phone and fit a new speaker.

Cause 6: A Software Bug After an Update

Sometimes the sound turns uneven right after a software update. A restart fixes most of these bugs, so switch the phone off and on again first. If the problem stays, check for a newer update, because the fix often comes in the next one. As a last step, you can reset the sound settings, but note down your settings first, because the reset clears them.

Cause 7: Your Hearing on One Side

This one is easy to miss. If one ear hears less well than the other, every phone will sound uneven to you. Test with another pair of ears, or swap the phone from one ear to the other during a call. If other people hear the phone fine and only you hear the difference, the phone is not the problem. A quick hearing check can settle it.

Quick Check: The Order to Try Things In

Work through these steps in order, and stop as soon as the sound evens out.

  1. Check the balance slider and set it to the middle.
  2. Restart the phone.
  3. Hold the phone with both grilles clear and test again.
  4. Clean the quiet grille with sound and a soft brush.
  5. Test with earbuds to see if the phone speaker is the problem.
  6. Check for a software update.
  7. If the quiet side stays quiet, test the speaker for damage or visit a repair shop.

Final Thought

Most of the time it is a balance slider, a hand over the grille, or dust in one side, and all three are free to fix at home. Work through the checks in order, clean the quiet grille with sound, and only visit a repair shop if the quiet side stays quiet after all of that.
