Laptop Speakers Crackling? Fixes for Windows and Mac

Your laptop speakers crackle, pop, or buzz, and music and calls sound rough. This is a common problem on Windows and Mac, and most of the time the cause is a driver or a setting, not a broken speaker. Work through these steps in order, and test the sound after each one.

Why Do Laptop Speakers Crackle?

Laptop speakers crackle when the sound signal gets broken on its way to the speaker. A bad audio driver is the most common cause, often after a system update. Audio enhancements can also distort the sound, and a wrong sample rate can make the audio stutter. Dust in the grille and a damaged speaker are less common, but they happen too.

The first job is to find out if the problem is the speakers or the software. Plug in headphones and play the same sound. If the headphones sound clear, the built-in speakers are the problem. If the headphones crackle too, the problem is in the software, and the steps below will help.

Step 1: Turn the Volume Down a Little

Play a song at full volume, then turn it down to about 70 percent. Small laptop speakers can distort at maximum volume, and that distortion sounds like crackling.

Also check the volume inside the app. A video player or a call app set to maximum can distort on its own, even when the system volume is fine. Keep both volumes below the top and test again.

Step 2: Restart the Laptop

Restart the laptop from the Start menu on Windows, or from the Apple menu on a Mac. A full restart clears stuck audio processes and reloads the sound system. This fixes more sound problems than people expect.

After the restart, play the same video or song that crackled before. If the sound is clear, the problem was a stuck process.

Step 3: Test with Headphones

Plug in headphones or earbuds and play the same sound. If the headphones sound clear, the problem is with the built-in speakers or their connection. If the headphones crackle too, the problem is in the driver or the settings, and the next steps will help.

Try Bluetooth headphones as well if you have them, and test a different file or video. A single bad audio file can crackle on any device, so make sure the problem is not just one file.

Step 4: Turn Off Audio Enhancements (Windows)

On Windows, open Settings, go to System, then Sound, and open the properties of your speakers. Look for Enhancements and turn them off. Bass boost and surround effects can distort the sound, and they are a common cause of crackling.

While you are there, open the Advanced tab and try a different default format. A wrong sample rate can make the audio stutter, and changing it is safe. Pick one, test the sound, and keep the one that sounds clear.

Step 5: Update the Audio Driver (Windows) or System Software (Mac)

On Windows, open Device Manager, expand Sound, video and game controllers, and right-click your audio device. Choose Update driver and let Windows search for a newer one. A driver that broke after an update is one of the most common causes of crackling.

If updating does not help, right-click the device again, choose Uninstall device, and restart the laptop. Windows will install the driver again on its own. On a Mac, there is no separate audio driver to update, so install the latest macOS updates from System Settings, then restart.

Step 6: Check for Bluetooth Interference

Turn off Bluetooth and play a sound. A nearby Bluetooth device can interfere with the audio, and a connected speaker or headset can steal part of the sound. If the crackling stops with Bluetooth off, a nearby device was the cause.

Also move the laptop away from other wireless devices for a quick test, because a nearby router or phone can add noise.

Step 7: Clean the Speaker Grille Gently

Look at the speaker grille in good light. If you see dust or lint in the holes, brush it gently with a soft brush and wipe the area with a dry cloth. Dust in the grille can make the sound rough, and it builds up slowly from daily use.

Do not push a pin or a needle into the holes, because sharp tools tear the mesh and can damage the speaker behind it. If your phone speaker crackles too, read our guide on why a phone speaker crackles for the same kind of checks.

Step 8: Check the Sound Settings on a Mac

On a Mac, open System Settings, go to Sound, and check the output device. Make sure the built-in speakers are selected, and set the output volume to a normal level. Also check that no sound effects or balance setting is pushing all the sound to one side.

You can also reset the NVRAM, which stores sound settings on a Mac. Apple publishes the steps on its support website, and they differ between Intel Macs and Apple silicon Macs, so follow the guide for your model. Restart the Mac after the reset and test the sound.

When It Is Time to See a Professional

If the sound is clear in headphones, the driver is up to date, the enhancements are off, and the built-in speakers still crackle in every app, the speaker itself may be damaged. A torn speaker or a loose part inside will not improve with settings. Take the laptop to a repair shop, and tell them what you already tried.

Final Guidance

Crackling laptop speakers are usually a driver or a setting, not a broken speaker. Turn the volume down, restart, and test with headphones first. Then turn off enhancements, update the driver, and check for Bluetooth interference. Only think about hardware when the sound is clear in headphones but still crackles from the speakers in every app.
