Phone Earpiece Speaker Not Working? Fix It

You put the phone to your ear, the call connects, but you hear nothing, or the voice sounds very quiet and far away. If your phone earpiece speaker is not working, the problem is usually small. Work through these steps in order, and test the sound after each one.

Earpiece or Loudspeaker: Which One Is Broken?

Your phone has two main speakers, and they do different jobs. The earpiece is the small speaker at the top of the phone, just above the screen. You hear callers through it when you hold the phone to your ear, and it plays at a low volume by design.

The loudspeaker sits at the bottom of the phone. It plays music, videos, alarms, and calls on speakerphone, and it is much louder. When your phone earpiece speaker is not working, music and videos may still sound fine.

Step 1: Raise the Call Volume During a Call

Call volume is separate from media volume on most phones. You can turn media volume up to full and still hear nothing on a call, because the call volume is set low. Make a call, then press volume up while the call is active.

If the voice gets louder, the problem was only the volume setting. If it is still silent at full volume, move on to the next step.

Step 2: Remove the Case or Screen Protector Covering the Earpiece

The earpiece opening is the thin slit at the top of your phone, just above the screen. Some cases have a raised edge that covers part of it, and some screen protectors are fitted too high and cover the slit. Even a small overlap can make callers sound muffled.

Take the case off and make a test call. If the voice sounds clear, the case was the cause. Check the screen protector too, and replace one that covers the slit with one that has a proper cutout.

Step 3: Turn Bluetooth Off

Your phone may still be connected to earbuds, a car stereo, or a smart speaker you are not using. When Bluetooth is on, the phone can send call audio to that device instead of the earpiece.

Turn Bluetooth off in the settings, then make a test call. If the voice comes back, a forgotten Bluetooth device was taking the sound.

Step 4: Clean the Earpiece Grille Gently

Dust and lint collect in the small earpiece slit over time. Pockets, bags, and makeup can leave a thin layer that blocks the sound, so the voice sounds quiet or muffled, even though the speaker itself is fine.

Brush the slit very lightly with a soft, dry brush, like a clean makeup brush or a soft toothbrush. Wipe away the dust with a dry cloth. Never push a pin, needle, or sim tool into the earpiece, because sharp objects can tear the mesh and damage the speaker behind it. You can also run a cleaning tone from our Speaker Cleaner, which shakes loose dust out of the grille with sound.

Step 5: Test Speakerphone Against the Earpiece

Make a call and switch it to speakerphone. If the caller sounds loud and clear on speakerphone but silent on the earpiece, the problem is in the earpiece itself. If the call is silent on both, the problem may be the signal or a setting.

This test tells you whether to focus on the earpiece grille and settings, or to check the network and the other person's phone instead.

Step 6: Restart Your Phone

A restart clears small software glitches that can silence the earpiece, and it takes less than a minute. Hold the power button and choose restart.

Make a test call after the restart. If the earpiece works again, a stuck app or a glitch was the cause.

Step 7: Check for Water

Water in the earpiece slit can block the sound, just like dust. Rain, a splash near a sink, or steam from a hot shower can leave moisture in the grille. The phone may look dry on the outside while the earpiece is still wet.

Dry the outside with a soft cloth and hold the phone with the earpiece facing down. Run the water eject tone on our Speaker Cleaner, or try the vibration mode, to help shake the water out. Do not charge a wet phone, and do not use a hair dryer, because hot air can push water deeper inside.

Step 8: Update Your Software

A software bug can break call audio, and phone makers fix these bugs in updates. Open the settings, go to the software update section, and install any update you find.

After the update, restart the phone and test a call. If the earpiece works again, the bug was the cause.

When It Is Time to See a Professional

If you tried every step and the earpiece is still silent, the speaker itself may be damaged. A loose connection, a worn speaker, or damage from a drop can only be fixed by opening the phone.

Tell the shop what you already tried, so they can start with the speaker itself. Do not open the phone yourself, because you can damage other parts.

Final Guidance

A phone earpiece speaker not working is often a small problem with a quick fix. Raise the call volume, check the case and screen protector, turn Bluetooth off, and clean the grille gently. Most earpieces work again after one of these steps. For more help, read our guide on how to test if your phone speaker is damaged, or see our fixes for a phone speaker not working at all.
