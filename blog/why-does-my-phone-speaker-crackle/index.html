Why Does My Phone Speaker Crackle or Distort at High Volume?

Why does my phone speaker crackle when the volume goes up, while it sounds fine at low volume? The cause is usually small, and a few quick tests will show you which one it is. Check each cause below, and test the sound after each step.

First, Isolate the Problem

Before you fix anything, find out where the crackle comes from. Play the same song in two different apps, then play a video, then make a short call. Turn the volume down to half and listen again. This takes two minutes, and it tells you a lot.

If the crackle follows the volume, the speaker or the grille is the likely cause. If only one song crackles, the file is bad. If it crackles in one app only, that app is the cause. If it crackles on calls too, the problem is in the phone itself. Once you know the pattern, the causes below will make sense.

  • Test with more than one app, so a bad app cannot fool you.
  • Test at half volume and at full volume, and note the difference.
  • Test music and calls, because they use different speakers.

Cause 1: Speaker Grille Blocked by Dust, Lint, or Debris

Dust and lint in the grille make the speaker work harder to push sound out. At low volume you may not notice it, but at high volume the extra strain turns into crackling and distortion. This is the most common cause, and the easiest to fix.

Brush the grille gently with a soft brush, and wipe the area with a dry cloth. Then open our Speaker Cleaner Tool and run the dust sweep, because the tones shake dust loose from deep inside the grille where a brush cannot reach.

Cause 2: Speaker Grille Covered by a Case, Skin, or Screen Protector Overhang

A case or a skin can cover part of the grille, and a screen protector can hang over the earpiece. At low volume the sound still gets through, but at high volume the blocked part rattles and distorts. Take the case off and play the same song at the same volume. If the crackle is gone, the case was the cause.

Use a case with a clean cutout for the speaker, and replace a screen protector that covers the earpiece slit. A small overlap is enough to cause the problem, so look closely.

Cause 3: A Poorly Encoded or Low Bitrate Audio File

A low quality audio file can crackle on any speaker, even a brand new one. The file itself is distorted, so no cleaning will fix it. This is easy to test: play a different song from a trusted app, or play a video you know sounds good. If the new sound is clear, the old file was the problem.

Download music from trusted sources, and avoid files that were converted many times. Streaming apps with a high quality setting sound cleaner than low quality downloads.

Cause 4: Aggressive EQ or Bass Boost Settings

An equalizer or a bass boost pushes the small phone speaker past what it can play cleanly. Extra bass makes the speaker move more than it should, and the sound breaks up into crackle at high volume. Open the sound settings and turn the equalizer off, then test the same song again.

Some phones also have a sound enhancement or loudness feature that does the same thing. Turn those off one by one until the crackle stops, and keep the setting that sounds clean.

Cause 5: The Volume Is Genuinely Beyond the Speaker Clean Output Range

Every phone speaker has a limit. Past that limit, the sound distorts no matter how clean the grille is. If the crackle only starts at the last one or two volume steps, the speaker is simply past its clean range. Turn the volume down one or two steps and the sound should turn clear again.

This is normal for small speakers, and it does not mean the phone is broken. A speaker that crackles at full volume but sounds clean at high volume is working as it should.

Cause 6: Speaker Damage from Water Exposure or Age

Water can damage the thin parts inside a speaker, and age can wear them out. A speaker that got wet, or one that has played loud music for years, may crackle at any volume. If the crackle stays after cleaning, and after testing with good audio files, the speaker itself may be worn.

Dry the phone fully if it got wet, then run the water eject tone. If the crackle is still there the next day, take the phone to a repair shop, because a worn speaker needs a new part.

Cause 7: A Software Bug or Driver Glitch

Sometimes the software is the cause. A recent update, a buggy app, or a stuck audio process can make the sound crackle even when the speaker is fine. Restart the phone first, because a restart clears the audio system. Then check for a software update, because updates often fix audio bugs.

If the crackle started right after an update, it is very likely a software problem. Wait for the next fix, or check the phone maker support page for a known audio issue.

Final Thought

Speaker crackling at high volume is usually dust, a case, a bad file, or a setting pushed too far. Test with different apps and volumes first, then clean the grille and turn off extra sound effects. Most crackling clears up with one of those steps. If it stays after cleaning and testing, let a repair shop check the speaker itself.
