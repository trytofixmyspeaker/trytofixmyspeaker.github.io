How to Clean a Bluetooth Speaker Safely

How to clean a Bluetooth speaker safely, without hurting the grille or the sound? Dust builds up on the outside, and water or dirt can get into the grille. This guide shows you the safe steps, in order, so your speaker sounds clear again.

What You Need Before You Start

You need very little to clean a Bluetooth speaker. Get a soft dry cloth, a soft brush like a clean makeup brush or a soft toothbrush, and a dry cotton bud for the small gaps. That is all. Do not reach for strong cleaners, bleach, or alcohol wipes, because they can damage the grille and the finish. Do not use a wet cloth that drips, because water running into the speaker is the one thing you want to avoid.

Step 1: Turn It Off and Unplug It

Turn the speaker off first, and unplug the charging cable if it is connected. Cleaning a speaker while it is on can make a loud noise if you touch a button by mistake, and water near a charging port is never safe. If the speaker has a removable battery cover, you can leave it on, because you do not need to open the speaker at all.

Step 2: Wipe the Outside

Wipe the whole outside with a dry soft cloth. Go over the top, the sides, and the bottom, and pick up the dust and fingerprints. For marks that do not come off dry, dampen the cloth very lightly, wipe the mark, and then dry the area at once with the dry part of the cloth. Never spray liquid straight onto the speaker, because it can run into the grille or the buttons.

Pay attention to the buttons and the seams, because dust collects there. A dry cotton bud can reach into the small gaps around the buttons. Press the buttons gently as you clean around them, so you do not push dirt deeper inside.

Step 3: Clean the Grille and Ports

The grille is the most important part to clean, because it is where the sound comes out. Brush it gently with the soft brush, in short strokes, and let the dust fall away. Do not press hard, and do not push a pin, a needle, or a toothpick into the holes, because sharp tools tear the mesh and can damage the speaker behind it.

Check the bass port too, if your speaker has one. It is the round hole that lets air move in and out. Brush around the edge, and do not stick anything deep inside it. If the port has dust inside, a gentle shake with the port facing down can help it fall out. Read our guide on how to clean a phone speaker grille for the same rules on a smaller speaker.

Step 4: Clean the Buttons and Charging Port

The charging port collects lint and dust, and a dirty port can make charging loose. Look inside the port in good light. If you see lint, pick it out very gently with a dry wooden toothpick, with a light touch, and only from the edges. Do not use a metal tool, and do not push anything deep inside, because the small pins inside the port can bend.

Step 5: Use Sound to Clear Water or Dust

If the speaker got wet, or if the sound is still muffled after brushing, use sound to clean the inside of the grille. Connect your phone to the speaker by Bluetooth, open our free Speaker Cleaner, and play the water eject tone through the speaker. The tone makes the speaker move, and the movement pushes water drops and loose dust out of the grille.

Turn the volume up, and let the tone run for the full time. If the speaker is a small one, keep the volume at a level that does not make it rattle. After the tone stops, play a song to test the sound. If it is better but not perfect, run the dust sweep once more. If the sound does not improve after a few runs, the problem may be a damaged speaker, and you should get it checked. Our guide on how to fix a water damaged phone explains the same first steps for a wet phone.

Step 6: Dry It Fully Before You Use It

Let the speaker sit in a dry place with good air for at least an hour before you use it again, longer if it got wet. Do not use a hair dryer, because hot air can damage the grille and push water deeper inside. Do not put it in direct sun for a long time, because heat can warp the plastic or the fabric.

When it is fully dry, turn it on and play a song at low volume first, then turn it up. If the sound is clear, the clean worked. If it still sounds muffled or distorted, repeat the brush and the sound clean once more, and if that does not help, get the speaker checked.

What Not to Do

Do not wash a Bluetooth speaker under a tap unless the maker says it is waterproof and gives a rating for it. Even then, do not use soap or a strong jet of water. Do not use compressed air up close, because it can push dust deeper into the grille instead of out of it. Do not open the speaker yourself, because the parts inside are glued or screwed in a way that is hard to put back, and opening it can void the warranty.

Final Guidance

Cleaning a Bluetooth speaker is simple when you go step by step. Turn it off, wipe the outside, brush the grille gently, clean the port with care, and use sound to push out water or dust that sits deeper inside. Let it dry fully before you use it again. A quick clean once a month keeps the sound clear, and it takes less than ten minutes. If the sound stays muffled or distorted after a good clean, the speaker itself may be damaged, and a repair shop can test it.
