How to Fix a Water Damaged Phone at Home

Learning how to fix a water damaged phone at home starts with one rule: act fast, but stay calm. The first minutes matter most, and the worst thing you can do is switch the phone on or plug in the charger while it is still wet. Follow the steps below in order, give the phone time to dry, and test it slowly at the end. Most phones that only got splashed or dropped for a second can be saved this way.

Step 1: Get the Phone Out of the Water Fast

Take the phone out of the water right away. Every second under water lets more water in through the speaker grille, the charging port, and the buttons. Do not waste time looking for a towel first. Pick the phone up, hold it with the ports facing down, and let the water drip out on its own.

Step 2: Switch It Off Straight Away

Switch the phone off as soon as you can. Power running through wet parts is what causes most of the damage, not the water itself. Press and hold the power button until the screen goes dark. If the screen does not respond to touch, hold the power button down until it forces off. Do not press buttons again and again to check if it works, because each press sends power through wet parts.

Step 3: Take Off the Case and Remove What You Can

Take off the case, because it traps water against the phone. Remove the SIM card and the memory card if your phone has them, and dry the trays with a soft cloth. If the back cover comes off easily on your phone, take it off too. Do not try to open the phone with tools. The goal is only to remove the parts that come off without force, so air can reach the wet spots.

Step 4: Dry the Outside With a Soft Cloth

Pat the phone dry with a soft, lint free cloth. Dab the speaker grille, the charging port, and the earpiece gently, and do not rub hard. Tilt the phone so water runs out of the ports instead of deeper inside. A dry towel works fine for this. Do not use a hair dryer, because hot air can harm the battery and push water deeper into the phone.

Step 5: Shake Out Water the Safe Way

Hold the phone firmly with the speaker facing down and give it a few gentle shakes. Short, soft shakes help water leave the grille and the ports. Do not swing the phone hard or hit it against your hand, because a hard knock can damage parts inside. After shaking, you can run vibration mode, which adds a gentle shake that can move water the hand shake missed. Wipe the phone again after each shake, because more water will come out.

Step 6: Use Silica Gel, Not Rice

Put the phone in a closed box or a sealed bag with silica gel packets, the small packs that come in shoe boxes and new bags. Silica gel pulls water from the air far better than rice does. Rice pulls in very little water, and rice dust can get into the charging port and the speaker grille and make things worse. If you have no silica gel, leave the phone in open air in a warm, dry room with good airflow. Never use an oven, a microwave, or a heater to dry a phone.

Step 7: Wait Before You Charge or Switch It On

This is the hardest step, because waiting feels like doing nothing. Wait at least 24 hours before you charge the phone or switch it on, and wait longer if the phone was fully under water. The inside must be fully dry before any power runs through it. Charging a wet phone can short the parts inside and turn a small problem into a dead phone. Be patient, and do not switch the phone on early just to check it.

Step 8: Look for the Water Damage Mark

Most phones have a small water damage mark inside the SIM tray. Take the tray out and look at the tiny dot or strip inside the slot. A white or light mark usually means the phone stayed dry inside. A red or pink mark means water reached the inside. This mark helps you decide what to do next. If the mark changed colour, be extra careful with the next steps, and plan a visit to a repair shop if anything acts strange.

Step 9: Switch It On and Test Everything

After a full day of drying, switch the phone on and test it slowly. Check the screen, the touch, the camera, the charging, and the sound. Play music and make a short call to test the speaker. If the speaker sounds muffled, water is still sitting in the grille, so hold the phone with the speaker facing down and run the water eject tool a few times. If the speaker still sounds wrong, work through our guide on how to test if your phone speaker is damaged before you decide on a repair.

When to Visit a Repair Shop

Some water damage needs a repair shop, and it is better to go early than to wait. Go if the phone will not switch on after a full day of drying, if the screen has lines or dead spots, if the battery drains very fast, or if the phone was in salt water, soapy water, or a drink. Those liquids leave salt and sugar inside the phone, and the phone needs to be opened and cleaned by someone with the right tools.
