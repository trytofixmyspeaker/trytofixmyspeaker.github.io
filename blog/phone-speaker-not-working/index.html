Phone Speaker Not Working? 7 Quick Fixes to Try

Is your phone speaker not working at all, with no sound from music, videos, or calls? Do not worry yet. Most speaker problems are simple, and these 7 fixes cover them in order. Try each one and test the sound before you move on.

1. Check the Volume and Mute Settings

Start with the simplest cause. Turn the volume all the way up with the side buttons, and check the mute switch and the do not disturb setting. On some phones the media volume and the call volume are separate, so check both while you play a song.

  • Press the volume up button while music is playing.
  • Check the mute switch and do not disturb mode.
  • Open sound settings and turn off any volume limit.

2. Restart Your Phone

A restart clears stuck audio processes and small software glitches that can silence the speaker. Hold the power button, turn the phone off, wait a few seconds, and turn it back on. Then play a song and listen.

If the sound comes back after a restart, the cause was software, and you are done. If the problem comes back often, check for a software update, because updates fix audio bugs.

3. Disconnect Bluetooth and Wired Accessories

Bluetooth earbuds, a car stereo, or a smart speaker can stay connected without you noticing, and the phone sends sound to them instead of its own speaker. A wired headset plugged in all the way can do the same thing. Open the Bluetooth settings and disconnect everything, then unplug any cable from the headphone jack or the USB port.

  • Turn Bluetooth off and test the speaker.
  • Unplug headsets, cables, and adapters.
  • Check the audio output setting if your phone shows one.

4. Clean the Speaker Grille

Dust and lint in the grille can block the sound so much that the speaker seems dead. Look at the bottom grille in good light, and brush it gently with a soft brush. Do not push a pin or a needle into the holes, because sharp tools tear the mesh.

You can also clean it with sound. Open our Speaker Cleaner and run the dust sweep, because the tones shake dust loose from inside the grille. If the dirt is stuck deep, try vibration mode next, because the shake can move what sound alone cannot.

5. Rule Out Moisture Damage

Did the phone get wet in the rain, near a sink, or in a steamy room? Water in the grille can silence the speaker, and water inside the phone can damage it. Dry the outside with a soft cloth, and hold the phone with the speaker facing down so water can fall out.

  • Do not charge a wet phone.
  • Run the water eject tone on our tool.
  • Let the phone dry fully before you test it again.

6. Update Your Software

A software bug can silence the speaker, and phone makers fix these bugs with updates. Open the settings, check for a software update, and install it if one is waiting. Also update the app that has no sound, because an old app can lose audio after a system update.

If the problem started right after an update, it is very likely a software issue. Restart once more after the update, and test the sound in more than one app.

7. Test in Safe Mode or Reset App Settings

Safe mode runs the phone with only the built in apps, so a bad third party app cannot interfere with the sound. If the speaker works in safe mode, an app you installed is the cause. Remove recent apps one by one until the sound comes back.

You can also reset the app settings, which clears the defaults and permissions for all apps without deleting your data. If the speaker stays silent in safe mode too, the cause is not an app, so move on to the hardware check below.

When It Is Genuinely a Hardware Problem

If you tried all 7 fixes and the speaker is still silent, the hardware may be at fault. A torn speaker, a loose connection, or corrosion from water will not improve with settings or cleaning. Back up your data and take the phone to a repair shop. Tell them the steps you already tried, so they can test the speaker itself right away.

Final Thought

A silent phone speaker is often a setting, a Bluetooth device, or a dirty grille, and those fixes take only a few minutes. Work through the list in order, and test after each step, so you know which one fixed it. If nothing brings the sound back, a repair shop can test the speaker and tell you the real cause.
