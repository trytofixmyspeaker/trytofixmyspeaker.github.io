How to Clean Speaker Safely at Home

How to clean a speaker without damaging it? The answer is the same for a phone, a Bluetooth speaker, and a home speaker: keep it dry, keep it gentle, and let sound do the deep work. This guide shows you the safe steps for each type, and the mistakes to avoid.

What Kind of Speaker Are You Cleaning?

The word speaker covers three very different things. A phone speaker is small, with a fine mesh grille and a speaker hidden behind it. A Bluetooth speaker is bigger, often with a cloth or metal grille, and it may have been outside in dust or rain. A home speaker is the biggest of the three, with a cabinet, a grille cloth, and a cone inside that you should never press.

The cleaning steps below start with the phone, because that is the speaker most people clean, and the one this site is built for. Then they cover Bluetooth speakers and home speakers, so the same guide works no matter which speaker you searched for. The tools stay the same throughout: a soft brush, a dry cloth, and sound.

What You Need

You need a soft brush, a dry soft cloth, and good light. A clean soft toothbrush works well, and so does a small makeup brush. The cloth should be dry, because a wet cloth can push water into the grille. You do not need any cleaner, any liquid, or any sharp tool.

If you want to clean deeper than a brush can reach, keep our free Speaker Cleaner open in your browser. Sound can shake out dust and water that sit inside the grille, where a brush cannot reach. For a phone, that is often the step that makes the biggest difference.

Step 1: Turn It Off and Take the Cover Off

Turn the speaker off first, and unplug a home speaker from the wall. Take the phone out of its case, because the case can cover part of the grille and hide the dust. Take the cover off a Bluetooth speaker if it has one, and keep the speaker on a clean surface while you work.

Look at the grille in good light before you start. If you see grey or brown dust in the holes, or if the sound is quieter than it used to be, the speaker needs a clean. Calls that sound far away and music that sounds flat are two common signs.

Step 2: Brush the Grille Gently

Hold a phone with the grille facing down, so the dust falls out instead of deeper in. Brush across the holes with short, light strokes. Do not press hard, and do not scrub in circles, because that can push the dust into the mesh instead of out of it. Brush the bottom grille first, then the earpiece slit at the top in the same gentle way.

For a Bluetooth speaker, brush the grille in the same way, with the speaker turned so the dust falls away from it. For a home speaker, dust the grille cloth with the brush, and wipe the cabinet with the dry cloth. Do not press the cone in the middle of a home speaker, because it dents easily and the dent changes the sound.

Step 3: Wipe with a Dry Cloth

Wipe the area around the grille with the dry cloth, to pick up the dust the brush loosened. On a phone, wipe the bottom edge and the top edge where the earpiece sits. On a Bluetooth speaker, wipe the outside of the case and the buttons. On a home speaker, wipe the cabinet and the edges of the grille cloth.

Do not push the cloth into the holes, because the cloth cannot fit and it will only push dust back in. If there is a sticky mark near the grille, dampen a small corner of the cloth very lightly, wipe the mark, and dry the area at once with the dry part of the cloth. Keep the damp part away from the holes themselves.

Step 4: Clean with Sound

A brush can only reach the outside of the grille. Dust and water that sit deeper inside need sound. Open our deep dust clean, turn the volume up to the top, and press start. The sweep plays tones that shake the speaker in different ways, so dust stuck in different parts of the grille gets shaken loose.

Hold the phone with the speaker facing down while the tone runs, and let it run for the full time. When it stops, play a song to test the sound. If the sound is clear, the clean worked. If it is still a little muffled, run the sweep once more. Sound works best for loose dust and fresh water. It cannot fix packed dirt, sticky grime, or a broken speaker, so do not keep running it if the sound does not improve.

How to Clean a Bluetooth Speaker

A Bluetooth speaker needs the same gentle steps, with two extra checks. First, check the charging port and the buttons, because dust collects there too. Brush them lightly and wipe them with the dry cloth. Do not push anything into the port.

Second, dry it fully before you use it. If the speaker got wet, run the sound clean with the speaker facing down, then leave it in a dry place with air moving around it. Do not use heat, and do not charge it while it is still wet. Read our full guide on how to clean a Bluetooth speaker safely for the step by step version.

How to Clean a Home Speaker

A home speaker is simpler to clean, because the parts are bigger, but the cone inside is delicate. Unplug it first. Dust the cabinet with a dry cloth, and dust the grille cloth with a soft brush. If the grille cloth comes off, take it off and clean it apart from the speaker, then let it dry fully before you put it back.

Do not use water or spray on a home speaker, and do not press the cone or the rubber ring around it. If the sound is still dull after a clean, check the wires at the back first, because a loose wire is a common cause. If the speaker crackles or makes no sound at all, that is a repair job, not a cleaning job.

Mistakes to Avoid

Never use a pin, a needle, a toothpick, or a SIM ejector tool to clean a speaker. Sharp tools tear the mesh, and once the mesh is torn, dust goes straight onto the speaker, and the sound gets worse. Never blow into the grille with your mouth, because your breath has moisture in it, and that moisture can leave dirt behind when it dries.

Skip canned compressed air too. It can push dust deeper into the grille, and the cold spray can leave moisture behind. A soft hand blower is safer, and sound is safer still. Never use a vacuum cleaner on a phone speaker, because the suction can pull at the mesh and the speaker behind it.

How Often Should You Clean a Speaker?

Once a month is enough for most speakers. Clean a phone sooner if you carry it in a dusty pocket, work outside, or go to the beach, because sand and dust get into the grille fast in those places. Clean a Bluetooth speaker sooner if you use it outside. A home speaker in a clean room needs less care, but a quick dust once a month still helps. A quick brush takes less than a minute, so it is easy to make it a habit.

Final Guidance

Cleaning a speaker is safe when you keep it gentle. Brush lightly with a soft brush, wipe with a dry cloth, and use sound to shake out the dust and water that sit deeper inside. Never use pins, water, or strong air, because those are the things that cause damage. Start with the phone steps above, then use the Bluetooth or home speaker section for the speaker you have.

If your phone speaker still sounds muffled after a clean, read our guide on how to clean your phone speaker grille safely, and our guide on how to clean tablet speaker grills for the same steps on a bigger device. If the speaker does not work at all, read phone speaker not working before you book a repair, because the fix is often a setting, not a broken speaker.
